# Laser-Cut Mathematics Manipulatives
## Design Guide & Reusable Kit Library (K–12 STEM)

> **Project:** Independent proposal — a laser-cut mathematics manipulative library for a K–12 STEM math lab
> **Machine:** xTool P2S (portable CO₂ laser cutter)
> **Purpose:** Turn abstract mathematical ideas into physical objects students can move, compare, assemble, measure, and test.

The best way to use a laser cutter is not to make decorative objects — it is to turn abstract mathematics into something students can hold and manipulate. Every manipulative in this library is designed to help a student **answer a mathematical question**, not simply to build an attractive item.

The xTool P2S is portable and does the job: it cuts and engraves the thin plywood, MDF, acrylic, and cardstock used throughout this library, and its enclosed design, camera alignment, and approved-material guidance make it well suited to supervised school production.

## 1. Laser-Cut Mathematics Manipulatives by Topic

### A. Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages

**Suggested kit: Fraction Lab** — create fraction circles, rectangular fraction strips, and percentage grids from thin plywood or classroom-safe sheet material.

| Manipulative | What students do |
|---|---|
| **1. Fraction circles** | Fit pieces into one whole circle to investigate equivalent fractions, e.g. ½ = 2/4 = 3/6. |
| **2. Fraction strips** | Compare and order fractions and model addition/subtraction using common denominators. Strips are divided into 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, and 12 equal sections. |
| **3. Percentage grids** | Cut a 10 × 10 grid with removable tiles or transparent overlays to connect fractions, decimals, and percentages (25/100 = 0.25 = 25%). |

**Challenge:** Find three different combinations of pieces that equal one whole.

**Mathematical learning outcome:** Students see *why* fractions are numbers representing quantities — not just symbols on paper.

---

### B. Functions and Algebra

**Suggested kit: Function Explorer** — functions are especially suitable for laser-cut manipulatives because students often struggle to understand the relationship between an input, a rule, and an output.

| Manipulative | What students do |
|---|---|
| **1. Function machine** | A board with three slots: **INPUT → RULE → OUTPUT**, plus number tiles and interchangeable rule cards. Put in 3, apply f(x) = 2x + 1, produce 7. |
| **2. Coordinate plane and graphing board** | A grid with holes or grooves at regular intervals plus movable points and line segments. Plot ordered pairs, construct lines, compare y = x, y = 2x, and y = 2x + 3. |
| **3. Algebra tiles** | Large squares (x²), rectangles (x), and small squares (units). Model expressions, expand products, and factor quadratics. |

**Activity (function machine):** Students predict outputs, reverse the process, and identify the rule from input–output pairs.

**Activity (coordinate board):** Change the coefficient or constant and physically reposition the graph to explore slope and intercepts.

**Activity (algebra tiles):** Build a rectangle representing x² + 5x + 6, then discover the factors (x + 2)(x + 3).

**Design tip:** Use distinct colors or engraved labels to distinguish variables, constants, and positive vs. negative quantities. For positive/negative algebra tiles, make them double-sided.

### C. Trigonometry

**Suggested kit: Trigonometry Explorer** — trig manipulatives should help students understand that sine, cosine, and tangent describe relationships among angles, sides, and coordinates.

| Manipulative | What students do |
|---|---|
| **1. Rotating unit circle** | A circle with coordinate axes, angle markings, and a movable radius arm, with holes/marks at common angles (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°). Rotate the radius and place markers for coordinates. |
| **2. Adjustable right triangle** | A hinged triangle with a movable angle and marked sides. Measure opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse, then calculate sin θ, cos θ, tan θ. |
| **3. Trigonometric graph kit** | A coordinate board with movable points or a removable sine-wave curve. Compare y = sin x, y = 2 sin x, and y = sin(2x). |

**Explore (unit circle):** Why is cosine the horizontal coordinate and sine the vertical coordinate?

**Explore (triangle):** As the angle changes, which side lengths and ratios change?

**Explore (graph):** How do amplitude and period change when the equation changes?

**Design tip:** Engrave degree markings and add a separate radians ring so students connect angle measurement to the unit circle and the periodic behavior of trig functions.

### D. Geometry and Measurement

**Suggested kit: Geometry Lab**

| Manipulative | What students investigate |
|---|---|
| Polygon tiles | Angles, symmetry, tessellations, and properties of shapes |
| Geometric nets | How 2D shapes fold into 3D solids; surface area |
| Tangram puzzles | Congruence, area conservation, transformations |
| Circle measurement kit | Radius, diameter, circumference, and π |
| Scale-model pieces | Scale factors, proportions, perimeter, and area |

**Activity:** Give students several geometric nets and ask them to **predict which will form a cube before assembling them**. They must justify predictions using the relationships among faces and edges.

---

### E. Statistics, Probability, and Data

**Suggested kit: Data and Probability Lab**

- **Probability spinner** — a spinner divided into unequal sectors. Predict the probability of each outcome and compare with experimental results.
- **Dice and sample-space tiles** — enumerate possible outcomes for two dice; investigate theoretical vs. experimental probability.
- **Physical bar graphs** — stackable tiles representing data values. Build graphs, compare distributions, calculate means and ranges.
- **Sampling model** — colored tokens for a population; draw samples and see how sample size affects estimates.

**Inquiry activity:** Run 100 spins, record outcomes, and compare relative frequency with theoretical probability.

---

### F. Calculus and Advanced Mathematics

For high-school advanced mathematics and introductory college courses, laser-cut kits can make otherwise abstract ideas tangible.

| Manipulative | What students do |
|---|---|
| **1. Riemann sum tiles** | Rectangles with adjustable widths and heights to fit beneath a printed/engraved curve. Estimate area using left endpoints, right endpoints, and midpoint approximations. |
| **2. Tangent and secant model** | A curve board and movable straight edges. Move two points closer together to investigate how the secant slope approaches the tangent slope. |
| **3. Conic sections** | Layered circles, ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas with movable foci. Investigate symmetry, distances from foci, and how shapes relate to equations. |

## 2. How to Design a Manipulative from Scratch

A repeatable design process for every mathematics topic.

1. **Choose the learning objective.**
   *Example: Students will explain how changing the coefficient of x affects the slope of a linear function.*
2. **Decide what students should manipulate.**
   For slope: a coordinate board, movable points, and adjustable line segments. The physical movement should demonstrate the mathematical relationship.
3. **Design the parts.**
   Use a CAD/vector program (Inkscape, Fusion 360, or the xTool software). Label parts and account for material thickness, kerf (cut width), slots, and tolerances.
4. **Cut and test a prototype.**
   Test fit, durability, readability, and whether students can use it independently. Adjust before producing a class set.
5. **Pair it with a challenge.**
   Every kit should include a question, instructions, a prediction, a hands-on investigation, and a short reflection or assessment.

## 3. Sample 60-Minute Mathematics Lesson

How to use a laser-cut function kit in a middle- or high-school classroom.

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | **Hook** | Show two function graphs. Ask: *What changed between them?* |
| 10 min | **Predict** | Students predict the outputs for different inputs using f(x) = 2x + 1. |
| 20 min | **Manipulate** | Use the physical function machine, input tiles, and output tiles to test predictions. |
| 15 min | **Extend** | Change the rule to f(x) = 3x + 1. Students explain what changes and what stays the same. |
| 10 min | **Reflect** | Students write the rule, draw the corresponding graph, and explain how the manipulative helped. |

> The laser cutting itself can happen **before class**. Students interact with the finished parts — not with the operating laser cutter — unless they are participating in an appropriately designed, supervised safety program.

## 4. Materials and Practical Considerations

| Material | Best used for |
|---|---|
| **Thin plywood or laser-compatible MDF** | Rigid, reusable tiles and boards |
| **Acrylic** | Transparent overlays, coordinate grids, and angle markers (when approved for the machine) |
| **Cardstock** | Inexpensive fraction strips, nets, and prototype pieces (when approved) |
| **Magnets, pins, fasteners** | Movable components — choose age-appropriate parts and secure small pieces |

### xTool P2S notes
- Portable CO₂ laser cutter with enclosed design and camera alignment — well suited to supervised school production.
- Always verify **material composition** and the manufacturer's **approved-material list** before cutting.
- **Never** laser-cut PVC or unknown plastics.
- Use proper ventilation, fire precautions, and trained adult supervision.

## 5. The Strongest Opportunity: A Complete Mathematics Manipulatives Library

Rather than producing unrelated classroom objects, build a standardized collection with a **reusable base board**, **interchangeable pieces**, and a **teacher guide** for each topic.

| Kit | Main concepts | Suggested grades |
|---|---|---|
| Fraction Lab | Fractions, ratios, percentages | Grades 3–6 |
| Geometry Lab | Shapes, angles, area, volume | Grades 3–8 |
| Function Explorer | Variables, input–output rules, graphs | Grades 6–10 |
| Trigonometry Explorer | Angles, unit circle, sine and cosine | Grades 9–12 |
| Data and Probability Lab | Statistics, probability, sampling | Grades 5–12 |
| Calculus Lab | Limits, derivatives, integrals | Grades 11–college |

> These grade bands are **starting points, not strict limits**. The same kit can be differentiated up or down by changing the challenge card that accompanies it.
